Product context

A CRM built for complex channel operations

EAPL Admin CRM was designed as a centralized system for teams managing dealer and distributor data, field visits, price lists, schemes, MOQ rules, order placement, points earned, feedback forms, product vendors, categories, and master data.

01

Scattered business data

Information was spread across multiple platforms, making it difficult for teams to access consistent and reliable data from one place.

02

Disconnected partner relationships

Dealers, distributors, regions, divisions, and departments were not centrally managed, making partner relationships difficult to track and maintain.

03

Complex business rules

Many business rules were hardcoded, making updates time-consuming and increasing the effort required to maintain the platform.

Product architecture

Breaking the CRM into clear modules

Instead of exposing every feature at the same level, I grouped the CRM into four functional areas so users could quickly understand where to manage data, where to configure rules, and where to track business activity.

Design system strategy

Creating a system before designing screens

Because the CRM had repeated table-heavy, form-heavy, and rule-based workflows, I defined reusable components and page patterns that could scale across modules without creating inconsistency.

Workflow 01

Helping admins manage dealer and distributor networks

Partner management became the base workflow of the CRM. The goal was to help admins quickly search partner records, understand associations, update details, and connect dealers with distributors, regions, and divisions.

01

Distributor Records

A centralized listing view that helps admins search, filter, compare, import, export, and manage distributor records from one place.

02

Distributor Profile

Profile gives admins a complete view of one distributor without making them jump across multiple pages.

03

Reward Ledger

A transaction-level view of reward point activity, designed to help track point credits, debits, and the business events behind each reward entry.

04

Dealer Mapping

This screen solves the problem of unclear distributor-dealer relationships by giving admins one place to see all associated dealers and update mappings when required.

05

Associate/ Edit Dealer

This flow helps associate a new dealer with a distributor while also updating dealer information in the same experience.

Problem

  • Partner data was dense and connected to many other workflows.
  • Admins needed quick scanning without opening every record.
  • Dealer-distributor relationships needed clearer visibility.

Design decisions

  • Used a table-first flow for faster record scanning.
  • Grouped details into tabs instead of long pages.
  • Added contextual actions for mapping, editing, and reviewing activity.
Workflow 02

Simplifying MOQ rule creation

A structured rule creation screen that helps configure minimum order quantity conditions across plant, product category, distance, amount, weight, and step based delivery rules.

01

MOQ Rule Listing

It helps teams quickly understand which rules exist, which product categories or plants they apply to, and whether any rule needs to be edited, reviewed or updated.

02

MOQ Rule Configuration

Define MOQ rules through a structured form and lets admins save progress as a draft before publishing

03

Reviewing Rules

Detail view that helps admins review MOQ conditions, monitor usage, track audit history, and take quick actions from one place

Problem

  • Admins needed a simple way to configure rules, review conditions, and feel confident before creating an active MOQ rule.
  • MOQ rule setup involved multiple operational conditions like product category, plant, warehouse type, distance range, and weight range.
  • Admins needed confidence before publishing active schemes.

Design decisions

  • Designed MOQ rule creation as a structured single-page form.
  • Grouped inputs into clear sections: basic rule information, warehouse rule slabs and notes.
  • Added a rule summary card so admins could review selected category, plants, warehouse type, and number of slabs before saving.
  • Added Save as Draft, Discard, and Create Rule actions to support both incomplete setup and final publishing.

Key learnings

What I learned

Dense workflows need clarity at every step

Even small admin actions can affect multiple workflows, so I learned to structure information clearly, use familiar patterns, and make each next step easy to understand.

Enterprise tools are more about reducing doubt than adding features

While working on EAPL, I realized that admins want to know exactly what they are changing, what the change will affect, and whether they are making the right decision. Small details like summaries, clear labels, status states, and review sections helped create that confidence.
